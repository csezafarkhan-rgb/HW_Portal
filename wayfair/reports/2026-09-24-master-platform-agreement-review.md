## 1. First thing to know

There is **nothing to sign**. The agreement binds you by conduct: registering, using Partner Home or
accepting purchase orders is acceptance. Wayfair can change the terms **at its sole discretion with 15
days' notice** (immediately for legal, regulatory, fraud or security reasons), and continuing to accept
POs after notice counts as accepting the new terms.

So the job is not "should we sign?" — it is "what has changed, what does it cost us, and what must we
put in place".

**What I could not verify:** Wayfair publishes only the current text, and I could not retrieve an earlier
version to compare, so I cannot produce a true list of changes. Ask your Wayfair account manager for a
summary of changes since the version you last reviewed, or check Partner Home → Change Log. The items in
section 2 are the ones that matter most, whether or not they are new to this version.

## 2. What to tell the team — the five points that matter

**1. We are bound by conduct, and the terms can change on 15 days' notice.**
Nobody signs anything. If Wayfair posts new terms and we keep accepting POs, we have accepted them.
*So:* somebody must actually read the Partner Home change notices. Today nobody owns that.

**2. There is a monthly platform fee per supplier ID.**
The amount is not in the agreement. Until we know it, we cannot price it into margin. At roughly
$21.5k of monthly revenue, even a few hundred dollars a month is material.

**3. Recalls are entirely on us.**
"Solely responsible for any Product Recall, including payment or reimbursement for any and all costs."
Ordinary liability insurance does not usually pay for a recall. This is the single largest open risk.

**4. Anything Wayfair deducts becomes final after 60 days.**
They can set off what we owe against what they owe us. If we do not dispute inside 60 days, it stands.
This needs to become a monthly routine, not something we notice by accident.

**5. Our product content can be used to train AI, permanently.**
The licence is worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable and royalty-free, and covers derivative works.
It survives termination. Nothing to do about it commercially, but the team should know before we invest
in expensive photography or 3D assets.

**Also worth knowing:** only Wayfair can send a dispute to arbitration, in **Hong Kong**; our default
venue is Massachusetts. There is no cap on our liability. Either side can exit with 60 days' notice, but
a material breach gives us only **five business days** to fix it.
